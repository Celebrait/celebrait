# Research: UK card market — first scouting pass (Thortful)

*2026-08-17, first field trip of the occasion-first playbook
([[SCOPE_OCCASION_FIRST.md]] step 1). Method: read the live shop —
taxonomy, category copy, and the bestseller wall's own labelling. We
study the FRAMEWORK (how the market slices occasions, what tones sell,
how cards are named); we never copy designs — they belong to their
creators, and the point is conventions, not cards.*

## The accepted framework (Thortful's information architecture)

Every occasion is sliced four ways — this is the market's answer to
"what does a birthday world contain", learned from years of their
sales data, and it maps almost exactly onto what Aidan sketched:

- **RECIPIENT** — thirty-five slices: Her / Him / Dad / Mum / Husband /
  Wife / Girlfriend / Boyfriend / Sister / Brother / Friend / Daughter
  / Son / Grandma / Granddad / Great-Grand× / Uncle / Aunt / Niece /
  Nephew / Step-× / In-Law-× / God-×. Recipient-first is how the
  market thinks. (Validates recipient inflection, and shows how far
  the relationship ladder goes — in-laws and step-relations are real
  aisles.)
- **STYLE** — Funny / Rude / Cheeky / Cute / Classic / Pretty /
  Belated, with filter tags adding Naughty, From The Heart,
  Traditional, Arty, Love, Retro. This is the tone spectrum sold
  today; "funny" is the flagship (first link on the homepage).
- **AGE** — 1st, 18, 21, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, plus kids split
  boys/girls. Milestones are first-class aisles, exactly the SEO
  routes Aidan named ("21st birthday card" etc.).
- **POPULAR** — Bestsellers / Now Trending / New In / birth-month
  aisles / licensed collabs (Mr. Men; a Ladybird Books card sits in
  the bestsellers ©-credited).

## Commercial anchors

- ~41,000 birthday cards on the marketplace; from £2.50; 4-for-£10 on
  A5; 2nd-class postage from £1.10; same-day dispatch cutoff 7pm.
- Read for us: we will never win on price or speed against a £2.50
  marketplace card. The product is the PERSONAL one they cannot rack —
  which is exactly the interest-led/photo territory. £8.99 needs that
  story told plainly.

## What the bestseller wall actually says (90-card birthday wall)

Labels are the sellers' own (style — motif — the line):

1. **Unmasked profanity sells at the very top.** "A Wise Woman Once
   Said — Fuck This Shit And Live Happily Ever After" sits top-three,
   printed in full, no asterisks. Our asterisk house style is therefore
   a BRAND CHOICE (mantelpiece-safe, nan-safe), not a market
   constraint. Decision for Aidan per occasion world, not a fact.
2. **The age-roast is the mainstream funny-birthday seam.** "I'm Not
   Saying You're Old, But If You Were Milk I'd Sniff You" — bestseller.
   Our engine currently bans old-age jokes outside rude mode; for the
   BIRTHDAY world that default is against the market's number-one joke
   genre. The birthday research pack must decide where Celebrait sits
   on the affectionate-roast dial.
3. **A pure typography card sits in the top ten** ("Happy Birthday
   Colourful Typography"). Text-only cards sell at bestseller level —
   commercial validation for the typeled format.
4. **Warm sells beside rude.** Pretty florals "For Mum — Loving, Kind
   & Beautiful" and a cute ice-lolly pun share the wall with the
   sweary cards. The wall is tonally WIDE — no single house voice, the
   racks serve different buyers, which is the whole occasion-first
   thesis.
5. **Seaside-postcard innuendo is alive** ("I'm Just Pulling Out of
   Paddington" — vintage art, rude tag). Our cheek register is
   period-correct.
6. **Fandom is an official aisle.** Category copy name-drops Love
   Island, The Office, Taylor Swift, Oasis, ABBA, Disney; licensed
   collabs exist for the big properties. Interest-led cards are a
   recognised, merchandised behaviour — our engine's home turf.
7. **Photo Cards and Face Swap are style aisles**, not novelties — the
   market already teaches buyers that photo-personalised is a style
   choice. Relevant to the parked photo-as-illustration route.

## Method notes (repeatable per occasion)

Card tiles carry `alt` text encoding style — motif — full front line —
creator. One JS pull per category page yields a clean tone dataset;
lazy-loading limits a single pass to ~15-25 unique cards, so paginate
or scroll-loop for depth. Moonpig blocked/slow in the in-app browser
this pass — use web search or retry for their occasion structure;
Scribbler (edgy) and Card Factory (value) are the other poles worth a
pass when their occasion worlds get built.

## Milestone scan, first pass (18th vs 60th aisles, 2026-08-17)

The age bands are different WORLDS, not different numbers:

- **18th** — adulthood-threshold jokes ("Make Decisions Yourself",
  "Childhood Expired", "Legally Do Everything"); pun-masked swearing
  ("Ducking Hell"); UK-culture references (Gavin & Stacey); and a
  GRANDDAUGHTER aisle — at milestones the buyer is often a
  grandparent, not a peer.
- **60th** — era nostalgia leads ("The Road To 60 — 1966", champagne
  "Born in 1966", Elvis "Your Fifties Have Left The Building");
  affectionate-habit jokes, not decline jokes ("Power Nap",
  "Six-Tea!", a Tesco Clubcard parody); unmasked swearing sells here
  too ("How the F***?" — printed in full).

**The birth-year unlock:** "Road To X — [year]" cards are a
first-class milestone genre, and for OUR maker the birth year is
DERIVABLE the moment the brief says "60th" — current year minus age.
That is not an invented fact (the banned class); it is arithmetic on
what the buyer typed. The birthday prompt may use it; the
never-invent-a-date rule needs this one carve-out written carefully.

**Age-roast calibration by band:** at 18 the joke is the threshold,
at 60 it is the era and the habits. "Old" mockery as such is rarer on
the walls than expected — the roast is affectionate and specific, which
is exactly our house style's home ground.
